How it works

Type anywhere when you speak, without using your fingers.

Purr sits in your menu bar. Click into a text box anywhere on your Mac, like Slack, Notes, Mail or Messages, then:

1

Hold the hotkey

Right Option by default. Switch it to another built-in preset: a bare modifier, a combo like ⌃⌥ Space, or a function key.

2

Speak naturally

Transcription runs on your Mac’s Apple Neural Engine. Your voice is never sent to a server.

3

Release

Your words type into whichever app has focus. Turn on Smart Typing and they appear while you’re still talking.

Dictation, Smart Typing, a meeting and Voice Edit in under a minute. Every line on screen is what Purr really typed.

More than dictation

Meetings, edits and your own words.

Meetings and Voice Edit are off until you turn them on in Settings. Like dictation, they run on your Mac.

FAQ

Before you download.

Does my audio leave my Mac?

No. Recording, transcription, summaries and Voice Edit all run on your Mac, and there is no telemetry. Purr only goes online to download models and, if you allow it, to check GitHub for updates.

How fast is it?

With Parakeet, the default, your words land about 0.2 seconds after you let go of the key. With Smart Typing on, they appear while you’re still talking. Whisper is slower, because it starts only once you let go.

What languages can I dictate in?

English with Parakeet, the default. For other languages, switch to Whisper in Settings › Engine. It covers about 100 languages and can translate them into English. Pick the one you speak, since auto-detect can misread short clips. Smart Typing is English only, and meetings work in English and 27 more languages.

Does meeting mode record the other people on my call?

Yes. It records your mic and your Mac’s audio together, so the other people on the call are transcribed too. That needs macOS 14.2 or later; on 14.0 and 14.1 only your mic is recorded. When nothing plays through your Mac, like a meeting around one table, Purr tells the voices apart and labels them Speaker 1, Speaker 2 and so on.

I can’t see the icon in the menu bar.

On notched MacBooks the icon hides behind the notch when the menu bar is crowded. Hold Command and drag to reorder, quit a few menu bar apps, or use Ice / Bartender to manage overflow.

How big are the downloads?

Parakeet, the default, is about 450 MB and downloads during setup. The rest download only when you turn on what needs them: Smart Typing adds about 220 MB, meetings in other languages about 630 MB, and Gemma 4 E2B, for summaries and Voice Edit, 3.35 GB. Whisper models run from about 140 MB to 950 MB. Settings › Models lists each one and can delete it.

Does it work without internet?

Yes, once the models you use have downloaded. Everything runs on your Mac.

How is it different from Wispr Flow, Superwhisper or VoiceInk?

Wispr Flow transcribes in the cloud and has apps for Windows, iPhone and Android. Superwhisper and VoiceInk can run locally too, with AI modes and more model choices, but their full versions are paid. Purr is free with no word limit, trial or account, MIT-licensed, and types as you speak. When I played the same recordings into each app, Purr put the text on screen fastest. The side-by-side comparison has those results, the details and sources.

Can I change the hotkey?

Yes. Each mode has a few keys to pick from in Settings, like Right Option, F5 or ⌃⌥ Space for dictation, and dictation can also start and stop with a tap. You can’t record a key of your own yet.

Can I use it in any app?

Dictation works in any text field. Voice Edit reads your selection through Accessibility, which most Mac apps support. A few, like some web pages and Electron apps, don’t share it, so Voice Edit can’t see what you selected there.

How do I update Purr?

The first time Purr starts after setup, it asks whether to check for updates automatically. If you say yes, it checks GitHub once a day. You can also click Check for Updates in the menu bar or in About at any time. You see what changed before anything installs, and Purr checks that each update is signed by its developer before it replaces the app.

Why does the menu say Permissions Needed?

A permission Purr needs is off, usually because it was switched off in System Settings. Click Permissions Needed to see which one and turn it back on. Updates keep your permissions.

About

Why I built this.

I type for a living. Most days my hands are on the keyboard for nine, ten, twelve hours. A few months ago my wrists started aching partway through the afternoon, just enough that I started closing the laptop after dinner instead of finishing one more email.

I tried the usual voice to text apps. A couple of them work well. But they all wanted a monthly subscription, and they all sent my voice to a server before it came back as text. I’ve written enough software to know that, in practice, your voice sits on somebody else’s disk for as long as they feel like keeping it.

So I built Purr myself, over a few weekends that turned into a few months. It does the same job as those other apps, except everything happens right there on your laptop instead of on someone else’s server. It took a lot longer than I expected. But three months in, it’s the first thing I open every morning, ahead of Slack and email.

It’s free and open source, and your voice never leaves your Mac. It doesn’t ask for a subscription or an account, and there’s no telemetry phoning home in the background. The code is on GitHub if you want to see how it works or change something you don’t like.

I built this for myself. I’m putting it out because if your wrists ache too, you might need it more than you think.
