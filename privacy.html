Privacy

What we collect, where it goes.

Last updated: 3 November 2026

Short version

Purr runs entirely on your Mac. The app collects nothing: no servers, no analytics, no account system, and no way to see what you say into the app. There is nothing for us to leak because there is nothing for us to store. The website uses privacy-friendly, cookieless analytics that count visits without identifying you; details below.

What the app does on your machine

When you hold the hotkey, the app records audio through your microphone, runs speech recognition on your Mac, and types the result into whichever app has focus. Dictation audio is held in memory only for as long as it takes to transcribe. It is never written to disk and never sent anywhere.

Meeting mode saves each transcript as a Markdown file, with its summary if you asked for one, in the folder set in Settings › Meetings, by default ~/Library/Application Support/Purr/Purr Meetings. It saves the meeting’s audio as an .m4a file next to it only if you turn on Keep audio, or if transcribing fails, so the meeting isn’t lost. Those files live on your machine. We do not see them.

Summaries and Voice Edit run on your Mac too, with Apple Intelligence (part of macOS 26) or the Gemma 4 E2B model. Neither sends your text anywhere.

The only network requests Purr makes

  • Speech model downloads. During setup the Parakeet TDT v2 model (about 450 MB) downloads from Hugging Face. If you switch engines, Whisper models (about 140 MB to 950 MB) download from the same place.
  • Optional features. Each downloads its model once from Hugging Face, the first time you use it: Smart Typing the Parakeet EOU model (about 220 MB), meetings a speaker-separation model (about 20 MB), meetings in other languages Parakeet Ultra (about 630 MB), and your vocabulary’s names and terms Parakeet CTC 110M (about 100 MB).
  • Summaries and Voice Edit. If you use them without Apple Intelligence, or choose Gemma, Gemma 4 E2B (about 3.35 GB) downloads once from Hugging Face.
  • Update check. If you let it, Purr checks GitHub for a new version once a day, and whenever you click Check for Updates. The check fetches a small feed file; the request names Purr’s version and nothing about you or your Mac. If you install an update, it downloads from GitHub, and Purr checks that it is signed by its developer before replacing the app.

Purr makes no other outbound requests, and none of these carries your audio or your text. You can verify with Little Snitch or the macOS firewall.

What this website collects

Aggregate, anonymous traffic counts, nothing that identifies you. We use Cloudflare Web Analytics, which is cookieless and does not store your IP address, fingerprint your device, or build any cross-site profile. It records aggregate page views, referrers, and visitor country: enough to know which pages people find useful, and no more. No cookies, no consent banner, no third-party ad embeds. That data is processed under Cloudflare’s privacy policy.

Children

Purr is not directed at children under 13. We do not knowingly collect data from anyone, so there is no separate flow for minors.
