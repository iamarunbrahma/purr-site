Meeting notes

Meeting notes that stay on your Mac

How Purr’s meeting mode records, labels and summarizes a call, all on your Mac.

Purr’s meeting mode records your mic and your Mac’s audio together, so it hears everyone on a Zoom, Google Meet or Teams call without a bot joining. When you stop, it writes a transcript that says who spoke and, if you like, a summary. All of it happens on your Mac, and it’s free.

How do I record a meeting?

  1. Turn on Meetings in Settings › Meetings. It’s off until you do.
  2. Tap ⌃⌥ M to start recording.
  3. Tap ⌃⌥ M again to stop. Purr transcribes the meeting, labels the speakers and, with summaries on, writes one.

What do I get at the end?

A Markdown transcript for each meeting, with a label on each line for who spoke. With summaries on, a second file holds the summary, written in the meeting’s language. Turn on Keep audio to save the recording alongside them. Purr shows the new file in Finder when it’s done. Meetings are saved in Purr’s folder in Application Support, or in any folder you choose in Settings › Meetings.

Does it record the other people on the call?

Yes. It records your mic and your Mac’s audio output together, so remote voices are transcribed too. That needs macOS 14.2 or later; on 14.0 and 14.1 only your mic is recorded. When nothing plays through your Mac, like a meeting around one table, Purr tells the voices apart and labels them Speaker 1, Speaker 2 and so on. Recording rules differ from place to place, so let people know you’re recording.

Which languages work?

English and 27 more. Pick the meeting’s language in Settings › Meetings, and the summary is written in that language. Languages other than English need a one-time download of about 630 MB.

What writes the summary?

Apple Intelligence on macOS 26, or Gemma 4 E2B, a 3.35 GB download, on any version. Both run on your Mac.

How is this different from a cloud notetaker?

A cloud notetaker uploads your recording to its servers, and many send a bot into the call. Purr does neither: the audio, the transcript and the summary stay on your Mac, and it works offline. In exchange, it needs an Apple silicon Mac with macOS 14 or later, and it only records calls you’re on yourself. The comparison shows how meetings work in Wispr Flow and Superwhisper.
