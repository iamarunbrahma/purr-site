Offline dictation

Dictation that works offline on your Mac

What runs on your Mac, what each part downloads, and when Purr goes online.

Purr is a free Mac app that turns your speech into text with no internet connection. After its speech model downloads once, during setup, everything happens on your Mac: dictation, meeting notes, summaries and Voice Edit. Nothing you say is uploaded.

Does the Mac’s built-in dictation work offline?

Sometimes. Apple says dictation of general text can be processed on your Mac instead of on its servers, and that Keyboard settings shows whether it is on yours. Dictating into a search box isn’t covered [1]. Purr always runs on your Mac, in any text field, and adds meeting notes and Voice Edit.

What works offline in Purr?

All of it, once the models it uses are on your Mac:

  • Dictation in English with Parakeet (about 450 MB, downloaded during setup), or in about 100 languages with Whisper (140 MB to 950 MB, depending on the model).
  • Smart Typing, which types while you talk. It adds about 220 MB and works in English.
  • Meetings with speaker labels, in English and 27 more languages. Languages other than English add about 630 MB.
  • Summaries and Voice Edit, with Apple Intelligence on macOS 26, or Gemma 4 E2B (3.35 GB) on any version.
  • Names and terms you add for Purr to listen for (about 100 MB), plus fixes for misheard words and voice commands like “comma” and “scratch that”.

Each one downloads only when you turn on what needs it. Settings › Models lists them all and can delete any of them.

When does Purr go online?

Only to download models and, if you allow it, to check GitHub for updates once a day. There’s no account and no telemetry. Each update shows its release notes first, and Purr checks its signature before replacing the app.

Is offline dictation accurate?

In my tests, Parakeet, Purr’s default engine, got 3.8% of words wrong in everyday speech and 4.0% in hard audio with noise, accents and quiet speech. Wispr Flow, which runs in the cloud, scored 3.4% and 4.0% on the same recordings. Purr put the text on screen 0.22 seconds after I let go of the key. The comparison has the full results.

Which Macs does it run on?

macOS 14 (Sonoma) or later on Apple silicon, so any Mac with an M1 chip or newer. Intel Macs aren’t supported.
